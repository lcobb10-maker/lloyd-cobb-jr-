# The flower company has her name now

*Lloyd Cobb Jr. · Engineering field dispatch · November 15, 2026*

There was a flower company in the code before there was a flower company on the street.

We called the experiment Bloomverse. We were testing whether somebody could move a vase, change the petals, and send a little piece of beauty without getting trapped in a checkout flow.

Then the person behind the work became the point of the work.

**Michelle’s Flowers.**

A name that belongs to someone. A place for the kind of gesture people make when they don't know how to say everything they're feeling.

The change sounds small until you look at what it had to cross.

Earlier today, **ittoldem.com/partners was a 404**. The main store runs on Shopify. The interactive atelier was running as an isolated AWS prototype. I didn't want to replace the fashion storefront just to get a flower project in front of people.

So I kept the main store in place. I created Michelle’s Flowers as a published Shopify partner page and connected the independent floral atelier to it. I gave visitors the short address they should be able to remember:

**[ittoldem.com/partners](https://ittoldem.com/partners)**

Shopify redirects that address to **/pages/partners**, where the immersive floral experience takes over the screen. The page still carries the association with ITT TOLD’EM. The entire flower brand is now Michelle’s.

The evidence is more useful than the announcement. Automated Chrome opened the short address, followed the Shopify redirect, checked the full-width mobile embed, confirmed the real 3D viewer, generated the free digital card, and saved a Michelle’s Flowers-branded PNG. **Seven checks, seven passes.** A 1080 × 1350 flower card came out of the actual running site.

That still doesn't mean we're taking orders. Michelle hasn't signed off on physical floral fulfillment. The model isn't manufacturing-ready, and the conversational helper still isn't the independent AI florist we intend to build.

There's an infrastructure issue, too: the embedded AWS site currently rides through a temporary HTTPS tunnel. The Shopify address is stable, but the iframe's upstream URL will need a persistent origin before I would call this production-grade hosting.

I want recruiters to see that part as clearly as they see the picture.

Sometimes a build moves forward because you invent something. Sometimes it moves forward because you give the invention a name, connect it to a real domain, and test every step somebody else will have to take.

**This one has a name now.**

— Lloyd
